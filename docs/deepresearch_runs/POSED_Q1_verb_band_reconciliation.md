## The question

Learning-outcome verbs are routinely sorted into levels of Bloom's revised taxonomy — Remember,
Understand, Apply, Analyze, Evaluate, Create — using published verb lists. I need to know whether
those lists actually agree with one another, and whether the primary source authorises any of them.

## Strand 1 — the primary source

Does Anderson & Krathwohl's 2001 revision, *A Taxonomy for Learning, Teaching, and Assessing*,
itself print verb lists for each level of the cognitive process dimension? If it does, report how
extensive they are: are they a handful of illustrative examples inside the text, or exhaustive
tables? Quote the book verbatim on this, and give the URL of the document the quote appears in. If
you can only reach summaries of the book rather than the book, say so explicitly and mark this
strand unverified rather than reporting what the summaries claim.

## Strand 2 — the census of published lists

Find **at least eight independently published verb lists** that assign verbs to the six levels. Each
must come from a different publisher: no two from the same university or university system. Prefer
university assessment offices, teaching and learning centres, accreditation bodies, and academic
publishers. A page that reproduces a list without naming where it came from does not count. For each
list, record the publisher, the document title, the year if stated, and the URL of the page or PDF
that carries the list itself.

## Strand 3 — the matrix

For each of these ten verbs, record which level each list in Strand 2 assigns it to, or `absent` if
that list does not contain the verb:

    define · identify · describe · explain · discuss · calculate · allocate · route · design · evaluate

## Strand 4 — the empirical layer

Find published studies that **measured** agreement, rather than asserting it. Two kinds:

- studies comparing published Bloom verb lists against each other and reporting how much they
  disagree, including any that count how many distinct verbs are in circulation or how many appear
  at more than one level;
- studies measuring how accurately a level can be inferred from an outcome's wording at all,
  including automated classification of outcomes or exam items into Bloom levels, with the reported
  accuracy.

For each, give the finding with its number, a verbatim quote, and the URL hosting that quote.

## Strand 5 — the discipline layer

Engineering and ABET-facing outcome guidance: do any published lists or accredited-program documents
place the verbs `allocate`, `route`, `calibrate` or `diagnose` at a level? Report where they appear
and at what level, or report that they do not appear on published lists at all. That second answer is
a real finding, not a failure.

## Strand 6 — reconciliation

Only after the strands above. For each of the ten verbs, state the number of distinct levels it was
assigned across the lists you found, and classify it: `stable` if every list that contains it agrees,
`contested` if the lists split, `absent` if fewer than two lists contain it.

**Table 2 — the matrix.** Rows are the ten verbs, columns are the source ids from Table 1, cells are

**Table 3 — per-verb reconciliation.** `verb | distinct levels assigned | which levels | verdict

## Rules that decide whether this is usable

- Every claim carries a **verbatim quote** in quotation marks and the **URL of the page that hosts
  those exact words**. Not a canonical topic page, not the organisation's home page — the page the
  words are on.
- If you cannot produce both, mark that cell or row `unverified` and say what blocked you. A
  confident, plausible answer with no traceable quote is worse than an honest `unverified` and will
  be discarded.
- Blogs, SEO "Bloom verb list" pages, content farms, aggregators, mirror domains and document
  resellers are pointers, not sources. Use them to find a published list, then cite the publisher's
  own page. A list you cannot trace to a named publisher does not enter Table 1.
- A teaching-centre page **is** an acceptable source for Strand 2, because an institutional verb list
  is itself the object being studied there. It is **not** an acceptable source for Strand 1, where
  only the book counts.
- Report what the sources say. Do not tell me which list is correct and do not recommend a course of
  action.

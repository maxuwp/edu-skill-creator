## The question

When a course designer writes a learning outcome, good practice asks them to also state what evidence
would show the outcome was met. I need to know what the primary sources actually require of that
evidence statement, what university templates ask for, and whether "evidence that merely restates the
outcome" is a recognised failure with a name.

## Strand 1 — Understanding by Design, on evidence

From Wiggins & McTighe's own published material, not from summaries of it:

1. What does Stage 2 require of acceptable evidence, and how do they distinguish the evidence from
   the goal statement it serves?
2. What do they say about using "understand" as the wording of a goal? They are frequently cited on
   both sides of this; report what their own text says.

Verdict for each: `required-explicitly`, `implied-not-required`, `not-addressed`, or `unverified`.
Verbatim quote and the full URL hosting it for each.

## Strand 2 — what university templates ask for

Find **at least six independently published templates or guidance documents** — different publishers,
no two from the same institution — that ask a course designer to record how an outcome will be
assessed, or what evidence will demonstrate it. For each, report the **quality criteria the template
itself states** for that field: must the evidence be an observable student product or process, must it
differ from the outcome wording, must it name a specific instrument, is any criterion stated at all.

Table: `id | publisher | document title | year | what the template requires of the evidence field |
verbatim quote | full URL`

A template that asks for the field but states no criteria for it is a real and reportable finding.

## Strand 3 — the restatement defect

Is "assessment evidence that only rephrases the outcome it is supposed to evidence" a **named** failure
mode anywhere: in assessment or educational-measurement scholarship, in accreditation guidance, or in
published campus guides? If it has a name, report the name and who uses it. If it is described without
a name, report the description. If nothing addresses it at all, say so explicitly.

Verdict: `named-in-literature`, `described-without-a-name`, `not-addressed`, or `unverified`. Give
every source you find, with verbatim quote and full URL. **A finding of `not-addressed` is a
successful answer to this strand, not a failure, provided you say where you looked.**

## Strand 4 — constructive alignment and evidence-centered design

1. Does Biggs' constructive alignment require an explicit statement of evidence per intended learning

2. Does Mislevy's evidence-centered design require an evidence statement per outcome, and **at what

   field in a planning template.

Verdict for each: `required-explicitly`, `implied-not-required`, `not-addressed`, or `unverified`,

## Rules that decide whether this is usable

- Every claim carries a **verbatim quote** in quotation marks and the **full URL of the page hosting
  those exact words**. Not a canonical topic page, not an organisation's home page.
- If you cannot produce both, mark the row `unverified` and say what blocked you. A confident,
  plausible answer with no traceable quote is worse than an honest `unverified` and will be discarded.
- **Source tier differs by strand, deliberately.** For Strands 1 and 4, only the authors' own published
  work or its publisher counts — a summary of Wiggins, Biggs or Mislevy is a pointer, not a source.
  For Strand 2, an institutional template **is** the object of study and is therefore a valid source.
  For Strand 3, published scholarship, accreditation guidance and campus guides all count, but say
  which kind each source is.
- Blogs, SEO pages, content farms, aggregators, mirror domains and document resellers are pointers in
  every strand.
- Report what the sources say. Do not tell me which approach is correct and do not recommend a course
  of action.
